## Choose What Deserves Your Focus

Success is not just about how hard or how long I work. It is about being deliberate with where I put my time and energy. I try to identify the small number of things that will actually move me forward, focus intensely on them, and execute quickly. Working ten hours on the wrong problem can matter far less than spending two focused hours on the right one. The more selective I am with my attention, the less energy I waste on things that feel productive but do not actually create progress.


## Build an Edge That's Difficult to Replicate

The goal is to become someone whose value is not easily replaceable. If another person can do exactly what you do in the same way, eventually someone probably will, potentially faster or cheaper.

I think the better approach is to build advantages that compound over time. That could come from strong relationships, reputation, a unique network, or becoming unusually good at the intersection of several different fields. The combination matters because it becomes much harder for someone else to reproduce.

Following the same path as everyone around you usually makes you easier to compete with. The more unique your combination of skills, knowledge, relationships, and experience becomes, the harder it is for someone else to offer exactly what you can.


## Keep Yourself Free to Take Big Chances

I think one of the biggest advantages you can give yourself is the freedom to take risks. Most people focus too much on what they could lose and not enough on what they could gain. You will never make the right decision every time, so the goal is to make enough intelligent bets, learn quickly, and adjust as new information comes in.

Early in life, this can be especially powerful because the downside is often relatively small while the upside can be enormous. I like the idea of looking for asymmetric opportunities where being wrong costs relatively little, but being right could completely change your trajectory. When something starts working, you can gradually increase your commitment to it.

The danger is becoming so comfortable that taking risks starts to feel impossible. A predictable income, expensive lifestyle, and attachment to stability can slowly reduce the number of choices you are willing to make. Keeping your obligations manageable and your life relatively flexible gives you more freedom to follow unusual ideas, explore things before they are obvious, and pursue opportunities that may take time to prove themselves.


## Let Effort Become Your Advantage

I think working smart can take you far, and working hard can take you far too. But reaching the very top usually requires both. At that level, you are competing with people who are already talented, ambitious, and full of good ideas, so sustained effort becomes one of the things that separates you.

There are real tradeoffs to working extremely hard, and not everyone should want the same kind of life. But when you find something you genuinely care about, putting a huge amount of energy into it can be deeply fulfilling. Momentum compounds. The skills you build, the reputation you earn, and the opportunities that come from earlier work can make later progress much faster.

The important part is learning how to sustain that intensity without destroying yourself in the process. For me, that means working on problems I actually care about and surrounding myself with people I enjoy building alongside. Hard work becomes much easier to maintain when the work itself feels meaningful.

I also think there is a huge advantage to putting in disproportionate effort early. Effort compounds much like capital does. The earlier you build skills, stamina, knowledge, and experience, the longer you have for those advantages to accumulate.


## Keep Faith When You Have Nothing Else

I think one of the most powerful things you can carry through life is faith in something greater than yourself. Whether you call upon **Hashem, Jesus, Allah, or simply believe in a higher power**, having something greater to turn to can become incredibly powerful when you reach your lowest point.

There will be moments when everything seems closed off and you cannot see a path forward. I sometimes think about **Moses standing before the sea, with danger behind him and no obvious way ahead**. From a human perspective, it looked impossible. Yet faith meant continuing forward before the way was visible.

That is what faith can do in your own life. Prayer, those quiet conversations with God, and trusting that there is meaning beyond what you currently understand can keep you moving when logic alone tells you to give up.

And I think those moments make the comeback different. When you have been at your lowest, prayed when nobody was watching, and somehow found the strength to stand again, you return with a different kind of resilience. Sometimes your strongest version is built in the moments when you had no clear path forward and all you had left was faith.
